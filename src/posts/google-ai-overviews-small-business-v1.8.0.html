Search for something on Google today and there's often a written answer above the usual results. That's an AI Overview. Here's what it means for a small business website, and what you can do about it.

What AI Overviews are

AI Overviews are short summaries Google writes for some searches, with links to the pages it used. They tend to appear for questions ("how much does a website cost in NZ?") more than for simple searches for a business name. Google also offers AI Mode, a chat-style search that works the same way at greater length.

Both draw on Google's normal search index. There's no separate system to sign up for and no special file to add.

Do they mean fewer visitors?

For some searches, yes. If the summary fully answers a simple question, fewer people click through. But the searches that matter most to a local business usually aren't simple questions. Someone looking for a plumber, a photographer or a place to stay still needs to pick one, check prices and get in touch. Those people still click, and the businesses linked from the overview get seen first.

So the aim isn't to fight AI Overviews. It's to be one of the sources they link to.

What Google says helps

Google's own guidance is that the same things that help in normal search help in AI features. There are no extra technical requirements. To be eligible, a page has to be indexed and allowed to show a snippet in search results. Beyond that, Google points to the basics:

  • Let Google crawl your site. Make sure important pages aren't blocked and are listed in your sitemap.
  • Put important information in text. Prices, services and areas written as text, not only inside images or PDFs.
  • Give people a good page experience. Fast on phones, easy to read, clear about what to do next.
  • Use structured data that matches what's on the page. It helps Google understand your business, though it doesn't guarantee a feature.
  • Keep your Google Business Profile accurate. For local searches, Google often shows business details from Maps alongside AI answers.

One setting worth knowing about: Google-Extended lets you opt out of your content being used to train Google's Gemini models. Google says it doesn't affect whether your pages appear in Search or AI Overviews.

Write pages that answer the question

AI Overviews link to pages that answer the question clearly. For a small business, that usually means:

  1. A clear page for each main service. One page that tries to cover everything rarely gets picked for anything.
  2. Straight answers near the top. If people ask "how long does a bathroom renovation take?", say "most take two to three weeks" before the detail.
  3. Real numbers where you can share them. Price ranges, timeframes, service areas.
  4. A short questions section. Use the questions customers actually ask you.

Check how you appear

Google Search Console shows which searches your website appears for and how often people click. Clicks from AI Overviews are counted in the same reports. If you don't have Search Console set up, that's the first job. Every website we build has it set up in the owner's name.

The bottom line

AI Overviews reward the same thing good websites always have: clear, honest, useful pages that load fast and say exactly what you do.
