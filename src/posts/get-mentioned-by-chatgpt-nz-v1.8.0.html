Ask ChatGPT for "a good electrician in Cromwell" or "the best café in Wānaka" and it will happily name a few businesses. Here's how it decides, and what a New Zealand small business can do to give itself a fair chance of being one of them.

First, the honest part: no one can guarantee that ChatGPT will mention your business. Answers change from day to day and from person to person. But there are clear reasons a business gets left out, and most of them are easy to fix.

How ChatGPT finds local businesses

When ChatGPT answers a question about local businesses, it usually searches the web first. OpenAI uses its own search crawler and third-party search providers, including Bing. It then reads a handful of pages and writes a summary.

That means three things matter:

  1. Can it reach your website?
  2. Does your website clearly say what you do and where?
  3. Do other sources back that up?

Step 1: Make sure your site isn't blocking it

OpenAI runs separate crawlers for different jobs. The one used for ChatGPT search is different from the one used to collect training data, and you can allow one without the other. Some website platforms and security settings block AI crawlers by default. If yours does, ChatGPT may not be able to read your pages at all. Ask your web person to check the robots settings and any bot-blocking features.

Step 2: Get your site into Bing

Most New Zealand businesses only think about Google, but Bing feeds several AI tools, including parts of ChatGPT search and Microsoft Copilot. Bing Webmaster Tools is free. You can verify your website, submit your sitemap and check that your pages are indexed. If you already use Google Search Console, Bing can import your site from there in a few clicks.

Step 3: Write pages a summary can quote

AI tools pick up plain facts. Make sure your website states, in normal sentences:

  • What you do, in the words customers use ("bathroom renovations", not "transformative spaces").
  • Where you work. Name the towns: Cromwell, Clyde, Alexandra, Queenstown, Wānaka.
  • Who it's for, and what makes you different.
  • Prices or a price range, if you can share them.
  • How to contact or book you.

A short questions section helps too. If customers often ask "do you do weekend call-outs?", answer it on the page.

Step 4: Make your details match everywhere

ChatGPT cross-checks what it reads. If your website says Cromwell, your Facebook page says Queenstown and an old directory listing has a disconnected number, it has less reason to trust any of them. Use the same business name, phone number and service area on your website, Google Business Profile, Facebook, Instagram and every directory you're listed in.

Step 5: Collect reviews and real mentions

When asked for "the best" anything, AI tools lean on what other people say. Google reviews, mentions in local news, a listing on your business association's website, or a link from a supplier or client all help. Ask happy customers for a Google review and mention the service and town if they're comfortable doing so. One genuine local mention beats a dozen paid directory listings.

Step 6: Label your business for machines

Structured data, or schema, is hidden information on your website that spells out your business name, area, phone number and services in a format machines read reliably. It doesn't force anything to happen, but it reduces the chance of a tool getting your details wrong.

Step 7: Check what ChatGPT says now

Ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Copilot the questions your customers would ask, such as "who builds websites in Central Otago?" or "what physios are in Cromwell?". Note who gets mentioned and which sources are cited. If a competitor keeps appearing because of a particular directory or article, that tells you where to get listed. Repeat every few months; answers change.

What not to do

  • Don't pay for "guaranteed ChatGPT rankings". Nobody controls the answers.
  • Don't stuff pages with town names and keywords. It reads badly to people and gives AI tools nothing useful.
  • Don't post fake reviews. Apart from breaching platform rules and the Fair Trading Act, they're easy to spot.

The short version

Let the crawlers in, get indexed in Google and Bing, write plain facts about what you do and where, keep your details consistent, and earn genuine mentions.
